# The window

Everything the window offers, view by view. The [README](../README.md) has the short tour.

It is written as on macOS. On Linux the window keeps its window manager's title bar, with the menus below it; Ctrl takes ⌘'s place in every shortcut; Quit is in File; Finder is the file manager; and what is macOS's alone - the Dock's badge and menu, File > Open Recent, the Window menu's list of projects - is left out: the welcome screen's recent projects and the project switcher (Ctrl+Shift+P) do their work. The preview's terminal copies and pastes with Ctrl+Shift+C and Ctrl+Shift+V, as Linux terminals do, so Ctrl+C stays the shell's.

- **Start a new project** - on the landing page (or File > New Project…, ⌘N): name a folder and choose where it goes, then which models it runs on - turri's vendors as `turri init-config` offers them - each saying whether you are signed in to the provider its models come from. Not signed in, the sheet offers to sign in to it there and then, in the sign-in sheet, before the project is made. On Amazon Bedrock, whose models - every maker pi's catalogue has there: Anthropic and OpenAI, xAI, Meta, Mistral, Qwen, DeepSeek, Amazon's Nova and the rest - where they run - a geography, or your AWS region - and the two models themselves, picked from what Bedrock serves there: turri's own pair for Anthropic's and OpenAI's, the strongest to talk to and a cheaper one to code for the others, as turri offers them. A geography other than global and us says which `AWS_REGION` to set - `ap-northeast-1` for jp, say - as pi calls Bedrock in us-east-1 unless `AWS_REGION`, or the AWS profile signed in with, names another. A turri that knows any pair (after 0.1.9) writes it and refuses a model pi's catalogue does not have; an older one writes its own, and the app puts the pair picked in their place. turri writes its `config.turri.yaml` (`initConfig`, from its host entry), the project opens on those models, and the chat starts with what turri wrote - its models; the terminal's advice on signing in is left out - ready for turri to write the first \*\*\*plain with you. It opens on the conversation: the chat two thirds of the window, "no \*\*\*plain yet" in the third beside it, the files hidden - until its first \*\*\*plain is written, when the files and the chat's width are as you keep them. Drag the chat's edge or show the files before then and that one is yours at once; neither is kept as your own. A folder of that name with files in it already is left alone; a project given up at the models takes its empty folder with it. Then turri doctor's checks run on it, the models asked too, to see that they answer with your credentials - a word each, so it costs a little: out of sight as the project opens, their sheet over it only when something wants seeing to - nothing is said when all is well - or, when turri cannot start on the models chosen - no key for them, most often - in the new-project sheet itself, which says so and what to do, and tries again once it is seen to. The folder and its config stay either way.
- **turri** - the conversation, on the left (⌘J), as wide as you drag its right edge (double-click it for the width it starts at), hidden by the × in its header and brought back by the turri button at the left of the toolbar: turri writes the \*\*\*plain with you and regenerates the code from it. Replies stream in, tool calls show as what they do ("Edited backend.plain"), a pulse that ends is one line in the chat - what it was, how it ended, its time and cost - coloured by its kind as in the regen view, and a click opens it there, its report and \*\*\*plain with it, and when turri asks you something - `ask_user`, a confirm, a choice - it is docked at the foot of the chat in the message box's place, the options as buttons, and the conversation stays in view above it. While a skill of several phases runs, its phases are above the message box: the ones done ticked, the one it is in turning, and what that phase does. What you say while turri is still answering is in the chat at once, faded and marked as waiting, below what turri is doing - its reply and tool calls going on above it - until turri reads it - after its turn, or, while a skill runs, as soon as the skill's own agent does, between its steps: then it is said, in its place after them. A turn stopped - the stop button or ctrl+c - never hears it: it goes back into the message box, before what you were typing, as pi's terminal puts it back in its editor. A turri command - `/regenerate`, `/turri-config` - runs at once, even while turri answers, as it does in turri's terminal. `/pulses`, which opens turri's side panel in a terminal, shows the regen view here, and is not among the commands typing / offers. Type `/` for its commands, and `:` for the concepts the project's \*\*\*plain can name - each with what its definition says and where it is defined, the arrows to choose and enter or tab to take one - coloured in what you and turri say as the \*\*\*plain colours them; click one there and the \*\*\*plain that defines it opens on its definition, lit for a moment - a module's in the \*\*\*plain view, a template's as its file, a standard library template's in stdlib. Tab completes a file the files show, from the word before the caret - `ser` to `server.plain`, by the start of its path or its name - or, when the word fits several, offers them, the arrows to choose and enter or tab to take one; when it fits none, tab does nothing, the focus staying in the message box. Files can go with a message: drop them on the chat, paste them - a screenshot, or files copied in Finder - or choose them with the paperclip by send. Each is a chip above the message box, its × to take it off. An image goes with the message for turri to see - drawn smaller first if it is larger than the models take - and shows in it in the chat; any other file goes by its path, on a line of the message, for turri to read. What turri says is Markdown, shown as it is written - lists, headings, tables, code - a `plain` block, or one with no language that has a \*\*\*plain section in it, coloured as the \*\*\*plain view colours the \*\*\*plain, a `diff` as a pulse's is in regen, and the \*\*\*plain in a diff of a `.plain` file by its grammar too - links (to the browser), its escapes; raw HTML in it stays text. Under the message box, on the left, how full the conversation's context is - a ring and its percent, the tokens on hover - what the conversation has cost so far (its own model's calls; the pulses' cost is in regen), and the model it is with. Several chats can be open at once, as tabs - up to nine, as turri's terminal has them, or eight beside the sentinel, which has the ninth slot of its own (turri says so when a ninth is asked for): ⌘T, the + after the last tab or `/new` typed in the chat opens a fresh chat beside the others, and ⌘W closes the chat shown - its conversation gone, its pulses running on - or, with one chat left, the window. ctrl+tab goes to the next chat and ctrl+shift+tab to the one before, round from the ends - while the focus is in the chat; in the views it goes along them instead. The tabs are folder tabs in the chat's header: the one shown raised and open onto the chat below, each named by its first message ("new chat" until something is said in it), with what it is doing - a spinner while it works, ? while it asks you something, a dot when it said something since you last looked; click one to show it, its × to close it. They narrow as more open, as a browser's do; more than fit scroll, the one shown kept in view. Each chat is its own conversation - its history, model and questions, and what you typed and did not send - and goes on working while another is shown; they share the project's one work loop, so a pulse any chat starts is in the regen view, and its end is said in the chat that asked for it. A question a hidden chat asks waits in it, its tab marked, until you show it. ctrl+c is the terminal's, wherever the focus is while the chat is open - but in the preview's terminal, whose shell it is. At a question turri asks - `ask_user`, a skill's interview - it declines it, the turn going on, however many questions in a row; at one of the window's own confirms it dismisses it. With no question, it stops what the chat shown is doing - its turn, a skill or a tool in it, a `!` command - and nothing in the others; in a chat doing nothing it clears what you typed, and with nothing typed it ends turri's session, every chat with it, and the window closes - as ctrl+c leaves turri in a terminal. A second press straight after one that stopped something does nothing, so pressing twice to be sure ends nothing. On Linux, with text selected - or in an editor outside the chat - ctrl+c copies, as everywhere there. A turri that cannot decline a question (before its `decline`) says so, and the question is answered or skipped instead. The stop button under the message box only ever stops the turn. On a turri without its interrupt, ctrl+c stops a chat at work and ends nothing. On a turri that has one chat (0.1.16 and before) there are no tabs and no ⌘T. turri knows it is in the app, not a terminal: it is given the window's views - \*\*\*plain, work, preview - in place of the terminal's side panel and keys, and told at each turn which view and file you have open.
- **The sentinel** - turri's own chat, which every pulse that finishes goes to (turri's `docs/sentinel.md`): it checks how the pulse went and, with **release-after-pulse** on, tests what was released - in the preview, with the same browser and terminal tools the chats have, so the preview says "turri is testing" while it does. turri opens it in the background with the first pulse that finishes; its tab is the last in the strip, apart from the chats, by its eye, named sentinel, with a spinner while it works and a dot when it has said something new. The window never switches to it: click it to read what it checked, and talk to it there - how to test the software, what to leave alone. It closes like any tab, its × there even beside the person's one chat, and the next pulse that finishes opens it again; ⌘W closes it when it is shown. With it, your chat's turri says in a line that a pulse landed and the sentinel is on it, and does nothing more with it. What it finds that needs you is a **notice**: a card in the transcript of the chat you are in - or, while you read the sentinel's, the chat that asked for the pulse - edged red for an error, tan for a warning, neon for what is only said, headed sentinel · the module · its pulse, with what is wrong, what shows it (muted, monospaced) and what can be done, each a button: press one and that chat's turri is started on it, **say what to do instead…** for your own words (enter sends them, escape goes back), **dismiss** for nothing. It is not docked and blocks nothing - you go on talking - and one put in a chat you are not looking at waits there, its tab marked, the window staying where it is. There is one open notice a module: a newer one about the same module supersedes the older. Once it is over the card says how - accepted, answered in your words, dismissed, superseded by a newer notice - and its buttons are gone; drawn again, a chat has its cards and how each ended. When the sentinel is done with a pulse the chat says so in a line, in the sentinel's indigo - ✓ sentinel · app · pulse 3 when it holds, and nothing more, or ✗ sentinel · app · pulse 3 · found something wrong, in red, what it found under it and its notice saying what to do: sentinel opens the sentinel's chat at what it said of that pulse, app · pulse 3 the pulse in regen. **sentinel** in the project settings turns it off (and **models.sentinel**, among each role's model, gives it a model of its own - else it runs on the chat's, models.chat or harness-model, as turri sets it when its chat opens); then a pulse is told to the chat that asked for it, as before. On a turri without a sentinel (0.1.19 and before) none of this is there.

- **Files** - the project's \*\*\*plain as a tree on the right, as wide as you drag its left edge (double-click it for the width it starts at), hidden and shown again with ⌘B or the button at the toolbar's right: the \*\*\*plain files, each with its state and how much of it is built, the templates of the project's own they import, and the resources they link - and nothing else of the project: not `config.turri.yaml` (the gear has it), the test scripts, nor what turri keeps for itself (`plain_modules`, `dist`, `.turri`). Which those are is turri's graph's say: a resource the \*\*\*plain comes to link appears, one it links no more goes, and on a turri without linked resources in its graph (0.1.14 and before) the tree has the \*\*\*plain files alone. Folders are listed as they are opened and again when what is in them changes, and which are open is kept for each project. Any file that is not a \*\*\*plain opens as text - a `.plain` template coloured by turri's grammar, code (YAML, JSON, Python, JavaScript and TypeScript, shell, Markdown, HTML, CSS, SQL, TOML, Go, Rust, Java, Dockerfiles, Makefiles, diffs) monospaced and coloured in the \*\*\*plain's palette, by its extension - to edit and save with ⌘S; what turri generates is read only, and a file that is not text opens in its own app. Right-click a row to open it elsewhere, reveal it in Finder or copy its path. Below the tree, the project's settings from `config.turri.yaml`; **debug** is a switch, off by default, that goes through the chat as `/turri-config debug true` - on, turri keeps what it writes for debugging it: every agent's whole log and session, each pulse's log (in `.turri/jobs/`), the test runs' outputs. Off, those go as each pulse ends, and the ledger is what is left of a pulse.
- **\*\*\*plain** - the `.plain` file, coloured by turri's own \*\*\*plain grammar, with each functional spec's state in the gutter beside it: a filled pink mark when its code is built, a cyan one with two ticks when its conformance tests are written and pass too - a step past built, and the \*\*\*plain's dot in the sidebar turns cyan when every functional spec is, a spinner while it is being built, an empty one while it is still to build - the \*\*\*plain said once, with how much of it is built above it. While a pulse runs, the lines it renders - what its \*\*\*plain change adds, or the functional spec it builds - are lit in the \*\*\*plain and brought into view, and stay lit where they move to as the \*\*\*plain is edited meanwhile - a functional spec added above them. When turri edits the file from the chat it is taken in at once, or, over edits not yet saved, offered. Rest the pointer on a functional spec - any of its lines, or its mark - and its state pops up beside it: regenerating or conformance testing, with the pulse's stage and how long it has been at it, regenerated, regenerated and conformance tested, not regenerated yet, or new and not saved. Click a mark (a native menu) to regenerate that functional spec again (**regenerate 1**) or write its conformance tests (**validate 1**) - once it is built: one not built yet, new or changed, is regenerated, not again, with the others not built yet, as each is built on the ones before it, and its conformance tests wait for its code. Functional specs open collapsed to their first line, each showing how many lines it hides: click that, the line or the chevron beside its mark to open it, or Expand all to open them all - remembered for next time. The buttons go through the chat - Regenerate is `/regenerate backend.plain` - so there is one work loop, whoever asks. Regenerate asks how: on from where it stopped, for a module partly built - its functional specs not built yet, on top of what is, chosen first while there are any - what changed in the \*\*\*plain - the default, and \*\*\*plain > Regenerate What Changed (⇧⌘R) without asking; both offered only when the \*\*\*plain has changed since its code was built, as is the chat's "regenerate what changed" - chosen built functional specs again though they did not change, the whole module from scratch, or it and every module it requires from scratch. A module with nothing built yet has no code to start over or to regenerate again, and its functional specs are each built on the ones before: it is regenerated all of it, or with its whole chain from scratch when it requires others. From scratch deletes code - committed first, so its history keeps it - and starts at once; the marks follow at once - the module's history moving, a pulse landing or its code deleted, has them read again.
- **regen** - each codebase's plan, and every pulse live: its stage, what its agent is doing and for how long, what it cost. Its tab turns a spinner while any pulse runs - regenerating or conformance testing - and says ! when one fails while regen is not shown: showing regen takes the ! away and opens the newest pulse that failed, once, brought into view and focused. Cards opened stay open as regen is left and shown again. Every list in it is the newest first, top down: this session's pulses - queued, running and done alike - by when each was queued, and below them earlier sessions' runs, each with its pulses the newest first too. Each pulse is headed by its summary - ten words and 70 characters at most, as turri's planner is asked to write it, and cut to that if it is longer, the whole of it on hover. What the view says can be selected and copied (⌘C). Each pulse is coloured by its kind, as the \*\*\*plain view marks what it does: regenerate pink, conformance cyan - the planned ones too. Each pulse's card is closed, to its head - what it is, how it is going or ended, its time and cost - with nothing that changes as it goes on but its time; click anywhere on it - but investigate - or press enter or space on its head, to open it, and its head again to close it. A pulse the chat says ended opens when it is clicked there. Open, a card has a line for each part of the pulse, each closed until it is clicked open - and kept open, as the cards are, as regen is left and shown again: the \*\*\*plain it works on, one line for each file it comes from - its module's own, and each it takes from: a module or template it requires, the standard library - saying what the pulse does to it - regenerating, validating (a conformance run), regenerated or validated - then the file; while it runs, its **agent trace** - opened, its steps and its agents, by the turns they have taken and what they have spent - gone once the pulse has ended; with turri's debug mode on (`debug: true` in config.turri.yaml, or `TURRI_DEBUG`), its **report**, once it ended, for debugging turri and left out otherwise; its **\*\*\*plain gaps**; and its **failure** - turri's diagnosis, and what failed that the work went on past. A failed one says so under its head, open or closed, with **investigate** right of it, which opens a new chat, shown, and asks turri there to find out why the pulse failed, explain it and fix what needs fixing, with what the pulse reported of it and the id turri looks it up by - an earlier session's failed pulse too. Its \*\*\*plain gaps are what the pulse found the \*\*\*plain does not say, each with what it assumed meanwhile. A file of its \*\*\*plain, opened, is the \*\*\*plain change it renders, coloured as the \*\*\*plain is, its lines lit and numbered among the sections around them, a long one scrolling in its own box - or the functional spec a conformance run builds. A click on its lines opens them in the \*\*\*plain view, lit, found where they are now when the \*\*\*plain has changed since the pulse ran, with the caret on the line clicked: a blank line found by the text beside it, a line taken out since where it was among the lines around it, a functional spec folded over it opened. They open so only while they are still there - not for a file gone since, or lines edited away - and a click that ends a selection leaves them be, to copy. Halting a pulse is turri's alone: the window has no Halt or Stop for one. Should turri's engine stop under the window - crash - its pulses are marked stopped, and a notice says so until **start it again** starts a new one on the project. When something fails that the pulse goes on past - its debugger or diagnosis, the model provider for good, a commit of its landed work - it is said once in a notice, naming the pulse, however often it fails, and marked on the pulse's card. Below this session's pulses are earlier sessions', from turri's ledger (`.turri/ledger/`), grouped by run - when it started, and how: in the app, in turri's terminal chat, a headless regenerate - the last three, and more on asking: each pulse's module and kind, how it ended - landed or not, who stopped it, or that its session stopped under it - how long it ran and its cost, and the same marks - and, open, the \*\*\*plain it regenerated, as turri's ledger keeps it (its `job.diff`, as last steered), its lines opening in the \*\*\*plain view as this session's do: a pulse recorded by a turri before it kept them has none. They are read only; one with nothing more than its head says - no \*\*\*plain kept of it, nothing failed - does not open. Ask turri about one in the chat: it looks it up by its id.
- **preview** - the app you are building, tried in a browser or a terminal (⌘3) - whichever is in use. A project opens on neither: the preview says there is nothing to try yet, with a button to open either yourself. Each opens once turri uses it, or you do, and once both are in use a switch in the bar goes between them. The browser is a pane of the window: type its address - `localhost:8000`, or just `8000` - and use it as in a browser, with back, forward, reload and its developer tools. Each project starts with a fresh browser - no cookies, storage or cache of another project's, or of a session before - and nothing loaded: the address it had last is offered, to open with a click, as another project's app may answer there now. The page loads again when a pulse lands. turri sees and tries it too: the chat has browser tools on the same pane - open a page of the app, read its text and controls, read its console, click - by a control's number, by the text it shows, or at a point of a screenshot, as your own mouse would, with another button, twice or with keys held - type and send a form, take a screenshot - and the window comes to the preview while it uses them, so you watch it work. They go only to this machine's own servers (localhost), never the web, and exist only in the app. A screenshot costs more than reading the page, so only the newest is sent to the model as an image; a model that turns images down - some do that their catalogue says take them - is sent none from then on, and turri carries on in the same turn without it. The terminal is a shell in the project's folder - your login shell, with your PATH - for a CLI: type in it as in any terminal, coloured in turri's palette, and ⟳ in the bar starts a new one; it is the same shell however often you switch away and back. turri has terminal tools on it too - run a command, type into what runs (a character at a time, for a program that misses fast typing), press any key or combination (ctrl+q, shift+tab, f5, pageup), read what it shows (with its rows and columns numbered, to aim at a cell), take a screenshot of it - as you see it, colours and all, for a terminal UI whose look matters; the same as the browser's, only the newest is sent as an image - - and, in a terminal UI that takes the mouse, such as a Textual or curses app, click on what it shows and turn the wheel over its lists, the cell marked a moment so you see where. A plain CLI takes no mouse: turri types and presses keys there. The window comes to the terminal while it uses them. Whichever of the two turri is in, it is edged in a glowing blue while turri works there, and its bar says "turri is testing" - until twelve seconds after its last action, so it holds while turri thinks between them. turri chooses which of the two to try something in by what it is: a CLI in the terminal, a frontend in the browser, a backend in the browser when it serves API docs to try it in (`/docs`, OpenAPI) and with curl in the terminal otherwise, anything else as it shows best; a server it starts in the terminal runs on there, for the browser to open.
- **stdlib** (\*\*\*plain > Standard Library, ⌘4 - not a tab among the views) - turri's standard library, to look through, read only: on the left every template by its kind - backends, frontends, command-line tools, libraries, languages - each with what it is, found by typing in its filter, and marked **in use** when the project builds on it, by importing it or a template that does; on the right the one chosen, as its \*\*\*plain, numbered and coloured as the \*\*\*plain view colours it, with what it imports, which templates import it and which of the project's \*\*\*plain files do - each a click away, and each module its \*\*\*plain imports a link too - and its conformance tests a tab away. ‹ goes back along the imports followed; **copy import** copies its name, to put under `import:`. From inside the project, every \*\*\*plain - in the \*\*\*plain view, or a `.plain` template opened from the files - says under its header what it imports and requires, and which of the project's \*\*\*plain files require it or import it: each a button, to go up and down the requires chain and into the templates - a module of the standard library opens here, the project's own in the \*\*\*plain view. Holding ⌘ underlines the modules its front matter names, its concepts and its linked resources: ⌘-click on a module opens it, on a concept its definition - the one that line names as its module sees it, in the \*\*\*plain itself, a template or the standard library, its line lit - and on a concept where it is defined, where it is used: a list at the click, each use's file, line and first line, to go to with a click or the arrows and enter, escape to put it away. On a linked resource, its file. What it links is as saved. All of it is turri's own reading - its standard library and its graph of the project's \*\*\*plain, from its host entry: on a turri without them (0.1.13 and before), the view and the links are left out, and so are the chat's concepts; one without linked resources in its graph (0.1.14) leaves out following those.
- **Preview the plan** (\*\*\*plain menu) - `turri glimpse`: the pulses Regenerate would run, and what the coding agent would be told, before anything starts.
- **Releasing** - with **release-after-pulse** on in the project settings - as a new project has it - turri releases what each pulse lands, as the pulse's last stage: the module's last commit, exported to `dist/<module>` and started on this machine. The sentinel then tries what was released, in the preview - in the terminal or the browser, as suits it - and puts what is wrong to you as a notice; with **sentinel** off, or on a turri without one, the chat opens it there instead and says how to open it and what to test, functional spec by functional spec. With it off, or to release again, ask turri in the chat: then it opens what it released and says what to test.
- **Check This Machine…** (Help menu) - `turri doctor`'s checks, from turri's host entry (`doctor`), run by the window's engine - so on the PATH turri runs with in the app, which is not always your shell's: Node, bash, git, the OS sandbox (Seatbelt on macOS, bubblewrap on Linux) and a model provider; with a project open, also each module's toolchains and what its testing scripts run. What wants doing comes first, each with what to do about it. **ask the models too** sends each model `config.turri.yaml` names a word, to see that it answers with your credentials - a request each, so it costs a little, and only on the click. Under what is missing, the way to it for someone who has never opened a terminal: on macOS, **install Apple's developer tools** - Apple's own installer, which brings git, bash's tools and a C compiler (`xcode-select --install`); on Linux, git's and bubblewrap's pages; a module's toolchain - Python, Node, Java, Go, Rust and the rest turri's stacks name - its makers' download page, in the browser; no model provider, the sign-in sheet. Installed something, **check again**; a tool still missing wants turri started again, as it reads your shell's PATH as it starts. The machine is checked when the welcome screen opens and the project when it opens, the models not asked: anything missing is said there, in amber - on the welcome screen under its buttons, in a project at the foot of the sidebar - and a click opens the checks. A turri without `doctor` (0.1.9 and before) says it cannot check.
- **Getting started** - until a project has been opened, just installed, the welcome screen leads the way to the first in three steps, each ticked once done: this machine has what turri needs - git, bash and the OS sandbox, what is missing with where to get it, checked again as you come back to the window - turri can reach a model, signed in to a provider, and your first project, started. Help > Getting Started shows them again, in a sheet over a project.
- **Project settings** (File > Project Settings… in the menu bar) - every setting of the project's `config.turri.yaml`, as turri's `/turri-config` knows them: the models and how hard each thinks, chosen from those turri can reach here, conformance tests and their budget, review, mutation testing, releasing after every pulse, the testing scripts, how many pulses run at once and how big each is, turn limits, the agents' environment, debug, what the project keeps - and, folded away, each role's model. Each says what it does and when a change takes effect, and is found by typing in its filter; a set one can be reset to its default. A change is sent to turri as you would type it - `/turri-config review false` - so turri checks it, writes it into the file with its comments kept, and says in the chat when it takes effect; the sheet reads the file again once it has changed. Open config.turri.yaml takes you to the file itself. On a turri that does not give them all - 0.1.12 and before - the sheet has the six the sidebar used to show.
- **Settings** (turri > Settings…, File on Linux, ⌘,) - saved as you change them, for every window: the colours and the text size (as View's), the models the new-project sheet has chosen when it opens - or ask each time - whether the app looks for its updates on its own and whether pre-releases count, with where they are and **check now**, which turri comes with the app, and what it sends. What a project runs on stays its `config.turri.yaml`'s.
- **About turri** (turri menu, Help on Linux) - the app's version and turri's, Electron's, Chromium's and Node's; **privacy**, what turri sends and where, also Help > What turri Sends, and Where… ([privacy](privacy.md) says the same); and the **third-party notices**, Help > Third-Party Notices…: every package the app carries, each with its licence, its text a click away and searchable, and Chromium's licences in a page of their own.
- **Sign In to Model Providers…** (File menu) - pi's sign-ins, as `/login` runs them in turri in a terminal, in a sheet of the window's: every model provider pi knows - forty or so - searchable, the ones this project's models come from first, then those with a subscription to sign in with, then API keys. Each says whether it is connected and how: signed in, an API key saved, from an environment variable (it names it), from pi's `models.json`. Choose one: a provider with a subscription and API keys both asks which - sign in with your Claude Pro/Max, say, or use an API key. A subscription signs in in your browser, which opens on its own: the sheet shows the address too, to copy or open when it does not, waits for the browser, and offers a field for the code the browser ends on, should it not reach the app - whichever comes first wins. The page the browser ends on is turri's, with its logo and colours: signed in, or why not, in the provider's words. GitHub Copilot gives a code to type on its page: the sheet shows it, and a button copies it and opens the page. An API key is a password field; Amazon Bedrock and Google Vertex ask first how - their choices as buttons. Cancel stops a sign-in at any point. What a sign-in stores is kept in pi's `auth.json` (`~/.pi/agent/`), where the turri CLI keeps it, for every project; one stored can be signed out of, while a key from the environment or `models.json` stays - it is not the app's to remove. With a project open, the sign-in goes through its chat's own model runtime, so the conversation has the credential at once - and a chat that could not start on `harness-model` without it is put on it. The sheet opens from the welcome screen (sign in to your models…) and the doctor's sheet too; `/login` typed in the chat opens it - `/login anthropic` on that provider - rather than going to the model as words, and turri is told to point you there. When a new project's models have no credentials, the new-project sheet offers to sign in to each of their providers, and opens the project again once you have.
- A window for each project, as many open as you like, each with its own engine, work loop, chat, preview and terminal. A project opens in the window in front when it has none yet - the welcome screen - and in a window of its own otherwise; one open already comes to the front instead, never in two windows at once. ⇧⌘N opens a new window. ⇧⌘P drops a list of the open projects from the top of the window, the one you were in last first - selected, so ⇧⌘P and enter go back to it: type to filter them by name or folder, and enter, or a click, brings that project's window forward. Or go from one to another as with any Mac app: the Window menu lists them by project, ⌘` goes to the next, and the Dock lists them too.
- **Updates** - the app updates itself from its releases: a new one downloads on its own, and once it is ready turri > Restart to Update (Help on Linux) restarts into it - or it is installed when the app next quits; while pulses run, restarting asks first. turri > Check for Updates… asks now. In the settings, the app can be kept from looking on its own - then only Check for Updates… looks - and take pre-releases too. turri comes with the app, so a new turri arrives this way too. A build not signed with a Developer ID on macOS, or the app run from source, says it cannot update itself.
- Native menus and shortcuts (⌘N, ⇧⌘N, ⇧⌘P, ⌘O, ⌘S, ⇧⌘R, ⌘1-4, ctrl+tab and ctrl+shift+tab to the view to the right and to the left - \*\*\*plain, regen, preview, round from the ends - while the focus is in them or they were clicked last, the preview's page and terminal too, and the chats while it is in the chat; ⌥⌘← (Ctrl+Alt+← on Linux) to move the focus to the chat - its message box, or the question in its place, opening it if it is hidden - and ⌥⌘→ to the view shown - the \*\*\*plain's editor, the regen list, the preview's page or terminal, ⌘B to hide or show the files - or the button at the toolbar's right, and hidden they stay - ⌘J, ⌘R to reload as in a browser - the page being tried when the preview's browser is in front, the window otherwise, which comes back on its project - and ⌘+ ⌘− ⌘0 to zoom the text, remembered), each acting on the window in front, recent projects - on the welcome screen too, each folder once by its full path, searched and scrolled past five - a dock badge counting the pulses running in every window, and progress on each window while its pulses run.
- turri's look: turri-dark by default, turri-light or the system's as chosen (View > Appearance). Every word the window shows can be selected and copied (⌘C), as in a document - but for a button's label, as a click presses it. the system's own fonts, nothing bundled: San Francisco for the interface and the ***plain, and Menlo - SF Mono where it is installed - for code, diffs and the terminal; rounded corners, fine lines and soft shadows. \*\*\*plain's own mark - its three asterisks, in its green (#79FC96, `public/plain/plain-mark.svg`) - beside a `.plain` file's name, in the file tree and where the \*\*\*plain view and a pulse name it - and the name itself, wherever the window writes it, is the mark then plain: in its own words and in turri's in the chat, though not in code, nor in the menus and tooltips, which hold only text. turri's pixel jellyfish, from its pixel kit (`src/pixels/`, `public/jellyfish/`): on the welcome screen and in the empty chat it grows from a polyp, sinks and grows again, then sways; small, its logo mark sits in the sidebar, and jumps in the chat, a pixel at a time, while turri works. The app's icon is its logo mark.
